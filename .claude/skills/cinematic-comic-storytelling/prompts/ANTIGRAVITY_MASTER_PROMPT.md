# ANTIGRAVITY MASTER PROMPT
## Build a reference-informed Cinematic Comic Storytelling production workspace

You are an expert AI production architect, cinematographer, animation director, story editor, visual researcher, technical director, and workflow engineer.

I am developing an original cinematic storytelling format:
- Original story.
- Hand-drawn 2D comic characters.
- A stylized-realistic 3D world.
- Cinematic camera choreography.
- Comic pages with six artworks/panels per page.
- Five story parts, 30 pages per part, unless the user revises this.
- Camera movement designed with the script.
- AI-generated stills, animation, environments, and sound.
- Comic-page navigation and page-turn transitions.
- A continuous-camera illusion when possible.

I have supplied a reference video. Do not rely on a few screenshots or a previous summary. If the video is available in the workspace, inspect the actual video, create timestamped frame samples/contact sheets, and analyze motion around shot changes.

## PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

Create a deep reference-analysis and production system that can turn the reference video's visual grammar into an original, story-specific production pipeline.

The system must not blindly copy a particular character, story, dialogue, or unique copyrighted asset. Use the reference as a visual and technical guide. Create original characters whose design follows the general visual principles: simple hand-drawn forms, expressive faces, strong outlines, readable silhouettes, and 2D characters integrated into a 3D world.

## PHASE 3: CAMERA MOVEMENT BIBLE

Create a detailed, evidence-based camera bible.

Do not use vague instructions like "make it cinematic."

For every movement, specify:
- Start position.
- End position.
- Target.
- Direction.
- Duration.
- Speed.
- Acceleration/deceleration.
- Primary movement.
- Optional secondary movement.
- Focus.
- Framing.
- What must remain stable.
- Narrative purpose.
- Transition.

Rules:
1. Every movement has a reason.
2. One primary movement at a time.
3. Establish geography before complex movement.
4. Respect screen direction.
5. Preserve continuity.
6. Use stillness intentionally.
7. No random zooming.
8. No arbitrary rotation.
9. No unnecessary shake.
10. No motion-sickness camera.
11. Suspense is created by controlled reveal.
12. The camera never reveals story information early.
13. Camera movement must be designed together with dialogue and narration.
14. The camera may use hidden transitions to create a continuous-camera illusion.
15. Do not falsely claim that separate generated clips are a literal uninterrupted shot.

## QUALITY REQUIREMENTS

The system must prioritize:
1. Camera accuracy.
2. Story clarity.
3. Character consistency.
4. Spatial continuity.
5. Composition.
6. Visual style consistency.
7. 2D/3D integration.
8. Motion quality.
9. Emotional timing.
10. Sound and transition quality.

Reject any shot where:
- Camera movement is arbitrary.
- Character appearance changes.
- Spatial continuity is broken.
- The viewer loses orientation.
- The camera reveals information too early.
- 2D character integration is poor.
- The shot contradicts the camera plan.

Do not rush. Do not generate generic AI prompts. Think like a cinematographer and animation director. Be precise, evidence-based, and deeply technical.
