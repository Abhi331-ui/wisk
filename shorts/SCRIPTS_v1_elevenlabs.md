# Five mystery shorts: ElevenLabs voice scripts (v1 + emotion tags)
Model: **Eleven v3** (audio tags only work in v3). Paste each block exactly as written.
Tags used: only energetic ones ([excited], [playful], [mischievously], [cheerfully], [amazed], [surprised], [curious], [sarcastic], [dramatically], [chuckles], [laughs]).
No silence-making tags: no [pause], [thoughtful], [sighs], [hesitates], [whispers], and no "..." ellipses or dashes, because they add gaps.
Suggested settings: Stability "Creative" or "Natural", speed 1.0–1.1. Target is ~3.7 words/s, so speed up if a take runs past 70 s.

---

## 1. The Tree That Grew Spaghetti
```
[excited] In 1957, the BBC convinced half of Britain that spaghetti grows on trees.
[cheerfully] It's April the first, and millions of families are watching the most serious news show on television.
[playful] The calm, trusted narrator takes them to a sunny Swiss village after a very mild winter.
[excited] This year, the spaghetti trees are having a bumper crop!
[cheerfully] A smiling family plucks long strands of pasta off the branches and lays them out in the sun to dry.
[playful] He even explains that the dreaded spaghetti weevil has almost disappeared.
[mischievously] And why is every strand exactly the same length? Generations of patient breeding, of course.
[excited] Then the phones at the BBC start ringing, and they don't stop!
[amazed] Hundreds of viewers want to know one thing: where can I buy a spaghetti tree?
[cheerfully] Luckily, it was an April Fools' joke.
[sarcastic] Unluckily, almost nobody in Britain knew what spaghetti was made of.
[curious] You see, in 1957 most British people had only ever seen spaghetti in one place: inside a tin, swimming in tomato sauce.
[playful] Think about it. If the most boring, trustworthy man you know told you pasta grows on trees, you'd probably believe him too.
[excited] And this was the most trustworthy voice on British television!
[playful] Some viewers were furious that the news had lied to them. [chuckles] Others just wanted their tree.
[mischievously] So the BBC gave them official gardening advice: place a sprig of spaghetti in a tin of tomato sauce, and hope for the best.
```

## 2. The Sandwich Thief
```
[excited] An entire office spent weeks hunting a sandwich thief who only ever ate the middle.
[cheerfully] Every morning, one employee puts a fancy homemade sandwich in the breakroom fridge.
[playful] Every lunchtime, the bag is still there, the box is still there, and the lid is even closed.
[surprised] But inside, there's nothing left except four perfect crusts!
[sarcastic] Management sends a stern email about workplace theft. Then a sterner one.
[mischievously] People start glaring at each other over the coffee machine.
[playful] Gary from accounts has crumbs on his desk. Gary is now the main suspect.
[excited] Finally, they install a security camera.
[dramatically] At three in the morning, a ceiling tile slides open, and a raccoon climbs down!
[excited] It pulls open the fridge, pops the lid, eats the filling, closes the box, and disappears back into the ceiling.
[cheerfully] Luckily, Gary was innocent.
[sarcastic] Unluckily, the thief had better table manners than Gary.
[curious] You see, a raccoon's paws work almost like little hands, and a huge part of its brain is devoted just to touch.
[amazed] Over a hundred years ago, a scientist gave raccoons thirteen complicated locks, and they cracked eleven of them!
[playful] Ever opened a jar in the dark without looking? That's how a raccoon sees the world, through its fingers.
[mischievously] To an animal like that, a fridge door held shut by a magnet is basically an invitation.
[cheerfully] The office fixed the ceiling, and the emails finally stopped.
[chuckles] But nobody ever looked at Gary the same way again.
```

## 3. The Bloop
```
[excited] In 1997, the ocean made a sound so loud that people thought scientists had found a sea monster.
[curious] America keeps secret underwater microphones scattered across the Pacific, built to listen for enemy submarines.
[dramatically] One summer, they pick up something new: a deep, rising roar from the far South Pacific.
[amazed] It's heard by microphones more than three thousand miles apart!
[excited] It's louder than any animal known to science, louder than a blue whale.
[playful] Scientists give it a name: the Bloop.
[sarcastic] The internet does not take it calmly.
[mischievously] Worse, it came from the same lonely corner of the ocean where a famous horror writer hid the sunken city of his giant tentacled monster.
[excited] Giant squid! A living megalodon! Cthulhu himself!
[surprised] Then, years later, scientists finally hear the exact same sound again, coming from near Antarctica.
[cheerfully] Luckily, the monster wasn't real.
[sarcastic] Unluckily, the truth was ice.
[curious] You see, when a giant iceberg cracks off a glacier, it's called an icequake, and it's incredibly loud.
[excited] Deep in the ocean, there's a layer of water that traps sound and carries it for thousands of miles.
[playful] It's like whispering into one end of a very long pipe. The sound can't spread out, so it arrives on the other side almost as loud.
[amazed] Even the spooky recording you can hear online is sped up sixteen times, because the real thing is too low for human ears!
[mischievously] So the most terrifying monster ever heard in the ocean turned out to be the same thing floating in your drink.
```

## 4. The Haunted Roomba
```
[excited] A family was convinced their house was haunted, because their vacuum kept cleaning at three in the morning.
[dramatically] Every night, while everyone sleeps, the robot vacuum switches itself on.
[surprised] It doesn't clean. It slams into the same wall, over and over!
[dramatically] By morning, there are zigzag scuffs across the floor, and everything in one corner is knocked over.
[mischievously] Like it's trying to escape something only it can see.
[playful] They check the schedule. Nothing. They reset it. It happens again.
[excited] So one night, they turn off the lights, hide on the stairs, and wait.
[dramatically] Three a.m. The vacuum beeps.
[laughs] And there, sitting on top of it like a tiny furry captain, is the cat!
[cheerfully] Luckily, the house wasn't haunted.
[sarcastic] Unluckily, the ghost still expected breakfast at six.
[curious] You see, robot vacuums have a big button right on top, and one paw is all it takes to start a cleaning run.
[playful] Cats are naturally most active in the dark, when they'd normally be out hunting.
[excited] And most of these robots don't really see walls. They find them by bumping into them and turning away.
[playful] It's like walking through your house at night with your arms out. Bump, turn. Bump, turn.
[mischievously] With a cat leaning on one side, every turn bends the same way, straight into the same corner.
[cheerfully] So the family did what any reasonable people would do.
[playful] They didn't get rid of the cat. [chuckles] They moved the furniture.
```

## 5. The Croissant in the Tree
```
[excited] In 2021, animal rescuers in Poland were called out to catch a dangerous creature that turned out to be breakfast.
[curious] It's Kraków, and a woman stares out her window at something brown and wrinkled sitting in a tree.
[dramatically] It has no head, it has no legs, and it hasn't moved for two whole days!
[playful] The neighbors are keeping their windows shut, just in case it flies inside.
[excited] So she calls the city's animal welfare society.
[dramatically] This could be an escaped reptile, an exotic bird, maybe something venomous!
[excited] An inspector shows up ready for anything.
[mischievously] Gloves on. Ladder up. Slowly, carefully, he reaches into the branches, and pulls out a croissant!
[cheerfully] Luckily, nobody got bitten.
[sarcastic] Unluckily, it had been out there so long that nobody wanted to bite it either.
[curious] You see, your brain is a pattern-finding machine, and when it isn't sure what it's looking at, it guesses animal first.
[playful] For our ancestors, mistaking a stick for a snake was harmless. Mistaking a snake for a stick was not.
[mischievously] Ever woken up at night and seen someone standing in your room, and it was just your coat on a chair?
[laughs] Same thing, but with pastry!
[cheerfully] Somebody had simply tossed it out of a window.
[playful] And the rescuers weren't even annoyed. They said she did exactly the right thing by calling.
[excited] So the wildest animal in Kraków that week was safely captured, and the only victim was the croissant!
```
